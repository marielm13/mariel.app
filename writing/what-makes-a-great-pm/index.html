What makes a great product manager

It's not an exhaustive list of hard skills or mastery of every framework. It comes down to three things.

I was recently asked in an interview what I think makes a great product manager.

In my opinion, it's not an exhaustive list of hard skills or mastery of every framework (no PM will tell you that PRD writing is what makes a PM great). It comes down to three things.

1.Being a clarity machine

A great PM can take messy inputs (research, feedback, stakeholder opinions, and ambiguity) and turn them into a strategy people actually understand. Not just what we're building, but why, for whom, and what we're not doing (and again, why).

This takes strong judgment and clear communication, especially since the information is almost never complete.

2.Knowing when to iterate vs. invent

This means seeing the big picture, recognizing patterns, and evaluating tradeoffs. It's understanding when the current solution needs refinement and when an experience needs an entirely new approach.

3.Soft skills (the unglamorous, silent majority of the job)

Product management is less about authority and more about influence. Much of the work involves navigating strong personalities, earning trust across teams, and aligning people with different incentives. It requires managing tension, resolving disagreements, and helping teams move forward even when there isn't a clear or perfect answer.

What makes product management especially hard

Most of this work isn't easily measurable. Soft skills and judgment can't be learned quickly or taught as theory.

You become a good (and eventually great) PM by doing the job: making decisions with imperfect information, getting some of them wrong, learning from the outcomes, and slowly building better instincts over time.
